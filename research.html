Scientific Directions

Research Focus

Current Focus Areas

Liquid-Liquid Phase Separation in Membraneless Organelles

We develop and deploy sequence-dependent analytical models alongside coarse-grained and all-atom molecular dynamics simulations to decode the molecular driving forces behind biomolecular condensation. By exploring how intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) transition between dilute and condensed dense phases, our work elucidates the thermodynamics of droplet formation. These investigations yield vital insights into cellular compartmentalization, stress granule assembly, and aberrant transitions underlying neurodegenerative disorders.

Protein Stability in Deep Eutectic Solvents (DESs)

We examine how deep eutectic solvents (DESs) and ionic liquids modulate macromolecular stability, folding dynamics, and enzymatic resilience. By probing the hydrogen-bond donor–acceptor networks and preferential exclusion phenomena in multi-component ionic mixtures, we unearth the microscopic mechanisms behind non-aqueous protein protection. This research offers essential foundational principles for green bioprocessing, whole-cell biocatalysis, and the long-term stabilization of therapeutics.

Machine Learning Interatomic Potentials (MLIP)

We develop and benchmark machine learning-based interatomic potentials (such as DeePMD-kit and equivariant graph neural networks like MACE) trained directly on ab initio MD data. This strategy circumvents classical force field limitations by retaining quantum mechanical density functional theory (DFT) precision at classical computational speed. These potentials unlock large-scale, long-timescale simulations of complex electrolytes and disordered interfacial matter.
